Notification Overview

RBI publishes a consolidated notification for Grade B (DR) recruitment — includes Phase I & II exam pattern, detailed syllabus and eligibility criteria.

Registration Window (Tentative)

Online registration opens after notification and remains active for a limited period. Exact dates will be listed in the official notification.

Exam Structure

Typical flow: Phase I (screening, objective) → Phase II (descriptive + objective for specific posts) → Interview (for final selection).

Vacancy & Posts

Vacancies vary by stream (General, DEPR, DSIM etc.). RBI publishes stream-wise vacancies and reservation details in the notification.

Recruiting Body

Reserve Bank of India (RBI) — Departmental Recruitment for Officer Grade 'B' (Direct Recruitment / DR).

Streams Covered

General (DEPR/DSIM specialized streams may have separate papers) — Grade B (DR) Officers across departments.

Selection Flow

Phase I (Online Objective) → Phase II (Objective + Descriptive for specific streams) → Interview → Final Merit List.

Eligibility & Qualification

  • Nationality: Indian citizen / subject of Nepal/Bhutan / person of Indian origin / Tibetan refugee as per RBI norms.
  • Age Limit: Varies by post and notification (typical lower & upper age limits specified with relaxations for reserved categories). Refer to the notification for exact cut-off dates.
  • Educational Qualification: Graduate degree (for General stream) from a recognized university. Specialized streams (DEPR, DSIM) require prescribed qualifications (e.g., postgraduate in economics/statistics/Maths/CS as applicable).
  • Experience: Not required for freshers in DR posts; check notification for stream-specific experience requirements if any.
  • Other: Candidates must produce valid documents, meet character/medical fitness standards and satisfy any language/local requirements listed in the notification.

Vacancy & Salary

RBI announces vacancies stream-wise (General, DEPR, DSIM etc.) along with category-wise reservation in the official notification. Vacancy numbers may change if RBI revises them.

Salary & Perks

Officer Grade 'B' pay scale and allowances are governed by RBI pay rules. Typical in-hand salary depends on posting/location and includes basic pay, DA, HRA, transport and other allowances. RBI officers also receive pension/contributory benefits as per RBI service rules.

Exam Pattern (Concise)

Phase I — Screening (Objective)

  • Objective multiple-choice online test covering: English Language, General Awareness, Quantitative Aptitude, and Reasoning/General Intelligence (sections & marks as per notification).
  • Duration usually 120 minutes (refer to notification for exact timings & sectional time limits).
  • Negative marking for wrong answers — check official scheme.

Phase II — Main (Objective + Descriptive)

  • Paper I: Economic & Social Issues / General Finance & Management / Statistics/Finance depending on stream.
  • Paper II & III: Objective/Descriptive based on stream (e.g., DEPR/DSIM have specialized papers; General stream includes Essay & English (writing) papers in some cycles).
  • Phase II carries higher weight in final selection; descriptive papers evaluated as per rubric.

Interview

Shortlisted candidates from Phase II are called for interview/personality test. Final merit is prepared using Phase II + interview scores (weights specified in notification).

Syllabus Highlights

  • General / Phase I: Current affairs (esp. economy & banking), English comprehension, quantitative aptitude, reasoning.
  • Economic & Social Issues (DEPR): Macroeconomics, Monetary & Fiscal policy, Inflation, Growth, Poverty & Unemployment, Balance of Payments, Financial sector reforms.
  • Finance & Management: Financial markets, Instruments, Financial accounting, Management principles, Corporate governance, Risk management.
  • DSIM / Statistics: Probability, Econometrics, Time series, Regression, Sampling techniques, Statistical inference and big-data/statistical computing concepts where applicable.
  • English / Descriptive: Essay writing, precise writing, comprehension and language usage (clarity & structure important).

Selection Process

  1. Apply Online: Submit application & pay fee during the registration window.
  2. Phase I: Objective online test for screening to Phase II.
  3. Phase II: Stream-specific objective & descriptive papers (marks used for shortlisting to interview).
  4. Interview: Personality test for candidates shortlisted from Phase II; check notification for weightage and qualifying marks.
  5. Final Merit: Prepared using Phase II + Interview (weights per notification); provisional allotment/offer issued to successful candidates.

How to Apply Online

  1. Visit the official RBI recruitment / careers portal and find the Grade B (DR) notification for 2025.
  2. Read the notification thoroughly — note stream-wise eligibility, exam pattern and document requirements.
  3. Register with a valid email and mobile number; complete the online application form with accurate personal and educational details.
  4. Upload scanned photograph, signature and any other required certificates in the prescribed format and size.
  5. Pay the application fee online (fee varies by category). Submit and save/print the application confirmation & registration number.

Important Dates (Tentative)

  • Notification Release: 2025 (check RBI Careers for official date)
  • Online Registration Start: Within days of notification
  • Last Date to Apply: A few weeks after registration opens
  • Phase I Exam: Typically 4–8 weeks after application window closes (exact date in notification)
  • Phase II Exam: Scheduled after Phase I results (dates announced on RBI portal)
  • Interview: Post-Phase II shortlisting (dates released for shortlisted candidates)

Note: Dates above are indicative. Always verify exact schedules on RBI's official notification before planning.

12-Week Preparation Plan (Focused)

  1. Weeks 1–3: Build fundamentals — brushing macro economics basics, quantitative aptitude, English reading & comprehension, and daily economy-focused current affairs.
  2. Weeks 4–6: Start stream-specific study (DEPR/DSIM/Finance) alongside practice questions & previous-year papers for Phase I & II patterns.
  3. Weeks 7–9: Timed mocks for Phase I, intensive concept practice for Phase II (write essays, practice descriptive answers), strengthen statistical/econometric tools for DSIM.
  4. Weeks 10–12: Full-length Phase II practice tests, answer-writing practice for descriptive sections, mock interviews and polishing general awareness narratives.

Recommended Books & Free Resources

  • Macroeconomics & DEPR — Mankiw (Principles), Agarwal's economics notes, RBI/IMF/World Bank reports for data & trends.
  • Statistics / DSIM — Casella & Berger (select topics), graduate-level statistics notes, time-series textbooks and econometrics primers.
  • General Awareness — Economic Survey, Union Budget summaries, RBI publications and monthly monetary policy statements.
  • English & Descriptive — The Hindu / Mint editorials for reading; practice answer-writing from previous RBI Grade B descriptive Qs.
  • Mock Tests & PYQs — RBI previous year papers, reputed online test series for time-bound practice.

Sample Practice Prompts

  1. Macro: Discuss the transmission mechanism of monetary policy in India and the role of liquidity adjustment facility.
  2. Quant / DSIM: Explain the difference between autocorrelation and heteroskedasticity. How do they affect OLS estimates?
  3. Descriptive (English): Write a 300-word essay on "Financial inclusion and digital payments: opportunities & challenges in India".

Tips & Common Pitfalls

  • Focus on depth for specialized streams — shallow coverage of DEPR/DSIM topics won't clear Phase II.
  • Practice answer-writing for descriptive papers under strict time limits — structure, clarity & data/examples matter.
  • Read RBI publications (Monetary Policy, Annual Report) for high-quality current affairs & authoritative facts.
  • Mock interviews: prepare to discuss your background, opinions on recent economic events, and questions from your written answers.
  • Keep documents scanned and ready; RBI's application portal expects exact formats for upload.
